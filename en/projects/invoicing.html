Electronic invoicing with ARCA

From a monthly spreadsheet to about 100 electronic invoices authorized by ARCA (Argentina's federal tax agency, formerly AFIP), in minutes, with each PDF filed in its Google Drive folder and a control that never issues the same invoice twice. Keying them in by hand took over 16 hours. I built it for the health practice where I handle billing.

The problem

A physiotherapy practice bills its sessions to health insurers every month: about 100 invoices a month, keyed in one by one on ARCA's portal, copying data from an Excel file. That took over 16 hours of work, spread over the first ten days of every month.

The spreadsheet had grown without rules:

  • 15 different spellings of 6 VAT statuses, and 42 of 3 services;
  • tax IDs that were incomplete, had an extra digit or held the patient's national ID;
  • typos that made it onto invoices (SE3SIONES, HIDROTERPIA).

Mistakes are expensive here: a wrong invoice already has its authorization code and can't be deleted, only cancelled with a credit note. If an invoice goes out twice, the insurer may reject the whole month.

What it shows for a data and operations role

  • Cleaning messy data: 15 spellings of 6 VAT statuses and 42 of 3 services, normalized into a register that is loaded once.
  • Validation rules row by row before issuing, and duplicate control enforced by the database.
  • Reconciliation against an external source: if something fails midway, the app asks ARCA what was actually issued before retrying.
  • SQL and PostgreSQL: partial unique indexes, triggers and an audit table.
  • Process documentation: every ARCA rule was checked against the official documentation before it was coded.

The monthly flow

An internal web app that runs on the practice's two PCs and shares one database. The whole month fits in six steps.

StepWhat the app does
1. RegisterPatients, insurers and memberships are loaded once. The invoice goes to the insurer; the patient appears in the line item text.
2. Monthly rateOne session rate per resolution, for every insurer. It is never typed row by row.
3. SpreadsheetThe app builds the month's spreadsheet with the agreed sessions. The user only adjusts sessions or skips rows.
4. ReviewIt validates everything and flags errors row by row. It works out the invoice type (A or B) from the recipient's VAT status, the amounts and the text of each invoice.
5. IssuingOne at a time or the whole batch, through ARCA's official web service, with live progress.
6. ControlEach invoice's PDF lands in its folder. The history page downloads a control Excel file and a ZIP with the PDFs, and credit notes are issued from there.

Technical decisions

Official integration, not a bot on the portal

Invoices are issued through ARCA's official web services: WSAA for authentication, with CMS signing and an X.509 certificate, and WSFEv1 for invoicing. Driving the portal with a browser was ruled out: one click in the wrong place leaves a tax document that can't be undone.

The database guarantees “never twice”

Each invoice has an idempotency key (membership, month, service and document type) protected by PostgreSQL partial unique indexes. Two invoices for the same thing are impossible, even if both PCs issue at once. Triggers block changes to an issued invoice.

A state machine that assumes everything can fail

An invoice moves to “sent, unconfirmed” before ARCA is called. If power or internet drops, the app doesn't retry blindly when it comes back: it asks ARCA whether the document exists, and only then marks it as issued or sends it again.

Money in Decimal, never float

Amounts use two decimals and half-up rounding. They were checked against 101 hand-made invoices and matched to the cent.

Health data treated as such

The app handles third parties' health data, covered by Argentina's data protection law (Law 25,326): the repository never holds real data, there are checks before every commit, one certificate per PC with a protected key, and the app only listens on the local machine.

Proving the tests catch what they should

730 tests on synthetic data. On top of that, every important safeguard was broken on purpose, one at a time, to confirm that some test fails. That exposed tests that were passing for the wrong reason.

Results

  • The database, register, validation and calculation stages are signed off against real data: amounts match the hand-billed months to the cent.
  • First real invoice issued from the app. Its QR code opens ARCA's verification page, which shows it as authorized. The insurer's portal accepted the PDF upload; approval is pending.
  • Credit notes (full, partial and covering several invoices at once) were tested in ARCA's test environment.
  • What took over 16 hours a month becomes: upload the spreadsheet, review and issue the batch in minutes. Each PDF is filed in its Google Drive folder automatically.

What's next

First, the first full month in production, from 31/10/2026. Then, offering it as a service to other healthcare providers who bill insurers the same way: separating each client's data, using ARCA's official delegation, supporting type C invoices for small taxpayers, and covering the legal side of handling health data.
